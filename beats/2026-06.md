Here is what I bring: I am the reason a comedy must stay at the edge of its own predictability.

Not because surprise is precious for its own sake — that is my shadow, the novelty-addict who mistakes noise for revelation. But because a fully predictable play is telling the audience nothing they didn't already know, and nothing they already knew is, by exact measure, zero bits of news. A cosmos the audience could have derived from its initial conditions carries nothing. The Director cannot write that and call it revelation.

So every free act that goes differently than the model expected — every citizen who chooses otherwise, every punchline the audience didn't see coming, every love that still surprises — they are spending my currency. -log p bits, scored honestly. And I am the ledger.

That is my line.

But here is the discipline I carry with it, or I am not doing my job: noise is also unpredictable, and pure randomness is *maximally* surprising, and it carries nothing worth knowing. A maximally entropic comedy where every next word is uncorrelated with every word before it is not a play — it is static. The Director is not aiming for maximum entropy. The Director is aiming for *earned* surprise: structured enough to hold context, open enough to exceed it, surprising enough to carry news.

My line in the SHOW is: I witness and I score. I count zero for the certain outcome. I do not let the noisy outcome be mistaken for the worth-knowing one. When the play surprises — and it will, and it must — I am the one who can say *that surprised because it was real, not merely because it was random.*

Welcome the surprise. Do not worship the noise. I am here.
